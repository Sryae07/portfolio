Who I am

I am Jiang Sirui, a computer science student at HaiNan Bielefeld University of Applied Sciences. I chose the degree because I wanted to know how the tools I use every day are actually built, and I have spent most of the first two years finding out the hard way — by running them.

My work tends to be the unglamorous kind: something that has to keep running overnight, a service that reports success while doing nothing, a document that opens but will not export. I like those problems because they have a verifiable answer. Either the socket is open or it is not.

How I work

I would rather finish four small things than start twelve. Before I call anything done I check it in the way a stranger would: fresh machine, no cached state, the links clicked rather than assumed. Most bugs I have shipped were found by that pass, not by reading my own code.

I keep notes on every project, including the dead ends. The work page has a "still broken" section for each one, because a project page that admits nothing is a page nobody trusts.
